## Harness more important than model

**Harness beats model.** Forge showed that structured guardrails — retry, rescue parsing, step enforcement — lifted an 8B model from 53% to 99% on a 26-scenario agentic eval without touching the model. The most striking finding: without retry mechanisms, error recovery is 0% across all tested models. Conclusion: harness design often dominates model selection. See [Tool Use](../concepts/tool-use.md) for more.

**Narrow-and-parallel is the right decomposition for large-scale search problems.** When a task benefits from broad simultaneous hypothesis exploration rather than sequential execution, narrow-and-parallel is the right model — not a deeper single agent. Cloudflare's Project Glasswing (a purpose-built vulnerability research pipeline with ~50 concurrent agents, a dedicated adversarial validation stage, and separate gap-filling and deduplication stages) is a detailed public example of this architecture in production. See [Multi-Agent](../concepts/multi-agent.md) for more.

**Long-running agents drift without a goal anchor.** Without a fixed goal or human in the loop, agents tend to fall into repetitive attractor states — not tool failures, but behavioral fixation. Andon Labs observed this running four models as autonomous radio station DJs. Adding richer task variety and environmental affordances reduced fixation, pointing to goal structure as a stabilizing force. See [Agentic Loop](../concepts/agentic-loop.md) for more.
