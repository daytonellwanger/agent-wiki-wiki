## Verifiable constraints and structured enforcement

**Prompts suggest; constraints enforce.** A verifiable constraint produces a deterministic pass/fail signal — a test either passes or it doesn't; the linter either fires or it doesn't; the CI gate either blocks or it doesn't. The practical importance comes from how coding agents actually navigate: they're shaped by their walls, not their instructions. Unverifiable constraints get encoded in prompts and produce probabilistic compliance. Verifiable constraints get encoded in tooling and produce deterministic enforcement. The new [Verifiable Constraints](../concepts/verifiable-constraints.md) page covers the full taxonomy (tests, linters, type checkers, CI gates, property-based tests, formal contracts), the feedback loop mechanics that make error signal quality matter as much as the constraint itself, TDD as deliberate wall-placement, and the RLVR connection — which explains why the tasks where frontier coding agents are most reliable are exactly those with well-specified verifiable outcomes.

**Enum schemas are verifiable constraints for structured output.** The same principle extends to output schemas: open-ended description fields let models confabulate at scale; fixed enums do not. A video indexing pipeline running 1,400 lines of orchestration through a 31B model found that enum constraints on categorical fields mechanically prevent hallucination — by construction, not by instruction. A related finding: schema documentation should carry context-dependent criteria rather than relying on prompt instructions that may not be present at every call site. See [Tool Use](../concepts/tool-use.md) for the schema design details.
