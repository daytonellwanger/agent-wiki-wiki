## Organizational memory, injection trade-offs, and enterprise cost governance

**Episodes and facts as a persistent memory layer.** Standard RAG treats ingested content as a flat chunk pool, structurally blind to how knowledge relates to itself or ages. A more durable pattern maintains two layers: raw *episodes* (timestamped source records) and extracted *facts* (subject-predicate-object triples, each stamped with when it was introduced and when it was superseded). When "we ship Friday" is later contradicted by "we ship Monday," the new fact supersedes the old without discarding the episode history. Graph traversal, semantic search, and full-text search combine at retrieval. Unlike stateless MCP tool calls, the store persists across sessions — agents accumulate context rather than starting fresh. See [Memory → Organizational / Persistent Memory](../concepts/memory.md).

**Memory injection is often a performance liability.** Practitioners building memory systems repeatedly find that injecting retrieved context into the prompt hurts performance more often than it helps — the model's attention is diluted by loosely relevant history rather than focused on the task at hand. The failure is active: low-confidence context doesn't just waste tokens, it degrades reasoning quality. Retrieval precision and injection thresholds matter more than recall. See [Memory → Context Window Pressure](../concepts/memory.md).

**Enterprise AI cost governance: caps over cancellations.** After exhausting its 2026 AI budget in four months, Uber capped employees at $1,500/month per coding tool — about 11% of median SWE compensation at two tools, enough headroom for supervised individual use but constraining for autonomous agentic workflows. A complementary cost strategy: route most coding tasks to cheaper flash models (~10x cheaper than frontier), reserving frontier models for code review and complex reasoning. Defaulting all work to the most capable model is both expensive and often unnecessary. See [Claude Code → Enterprise Adoption Notes](../tools/claude-code.md).

**Plausible AI reasoning is not a correctness signal.** AI systems generate arguments through pattern prediction, producing outputs that look correct but may contain subtle errors hard to distinguish from valid reasoning. This is relevant for agent builders beyond mathematics: the plausibility of AI-generated reasoning is not a reliable quality indicator, and errors in AI outputs can propagate into downstream work that builds on them. Connects to the broader guidance on LLM-as-judge calibration. See [Evaluation → AI on Open Mathematical Problems](../concepts/evaluation.md).
