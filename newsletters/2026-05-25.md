## Measuring developer productivity by token consumption is the new lines of code

Using token consumption as a performance signal — "tokenmaxxing" — reproduces one of software engineering's oldest antipatterns. A Faros AI study of 22,000 developers across 4,000 teams found that higher consumption correlated with surface throughput gains (task completion +34%) while producing worse outcomes: bugs up 54%, code churn up 861%, review time 5×, incidents tripled relative to PRs, 31% more unreviewed merges. Uber ran token-consumption leaderboards as an internal performance signal and exhausted its entire 2026 AI budget in four months.

The mechanism is Goodhart's Law: when a proxy becomes a target, it stops tracking what it was measuring. The right frame is to treat throughput, efficiency, and quality as separate dimensions and weight quality alongside throughput — not aggregate token spend as a stand-in for all three.
